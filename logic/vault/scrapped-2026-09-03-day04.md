## Scrapped Questions & Original Answers

### A1
- **Original**: Give a counterexample to: ``Every prime number is odd.''
- **Original Ans**: $n=2$.

### A2
- **Original**: Give a counterexample to: ``$n^2\geq n$ for every real number $n$.''
- **Original Ans**: $n=\frac{1}{2}$.

### A3
- **Original**: Give a counterexample to: ``If $n$ is prime, then $n+2$ is prime.''
- **Original Ans**: $n=7$ (or $n=2$).

### A4
- **Original**: Give a counterexample to: ``Every multiple of $4$ is a multiple of $8$.''
- **Original Ans**: $n=4$ (or $n=12$).

### A5
- **Original**: To prove ``there is no largest even integer'' by contradiction, what should you assume first?
- **Original Ans**: ``Assume there exists a largest even integer $M$.''

### A6
- **Original**: True or false: ``If assuming not P leads to something impossible, P must be true.''
- **Original Ans**: True.

### A7
- **Original**: Give a counterexample to: ``$\sqrt{a+b}=\sqrt a+\sqrt b$ for all $a,b\geq0$.''
- **Original Ans**: $a=1, b=1$.

### A8
- **Original**: Give a counterexample to: ``$(x+y)^2=x^2+y^2$ for all real $x,y$.''
- **Original Ans**: $x=1, y=1$.

### A9
- **Original**: True or false: ``A single counterexample is enough to disprove a 'for all' claim.''
- **Original Ans**: True.

### A10
- **Original**: Give a counterexample to: ``$n!+1$ is always prime.''
- **Original Ans**: $n=4$ ($4!+1=25=5^2$).

### B1
- **Original**: Five envelopes contain 6, 9, 14, 21, 25... which provides a counterexample to star-sealed even?
- **Original Ans**: Envelope with 25.

### B4
- **Original**: Prove by contradiction: ``There is no largest even integer.''
- **Original Ans**: Assume largest is $2k$, then $2k+2$ is larger even integer.

### B5
- **Original**: Give a counterexample (using $n=0$) to: ``For every integer $n$, $n/n=1$.''
- **Original Ans**: $n=0$.

### B6
- **Original**: Give a counterexample (using $n=1$) to: ``Every positive integer has at least one prime factor.''
- **Original Ans**: $n=1$.

### B7
- **Original**: Prove by contradiction: ``There is no smallest positive rational number.''
- **Original Ans**: If $q>0$, $q/2$ is smaller positive rational.
