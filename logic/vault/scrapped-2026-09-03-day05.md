## Scrapped Questions & Original Answers

### A1
- **Original**: Name the fallacy: ``If $n$ is divisible by $6$, then $n$ is divisible by $3$. $n=15$ is divisible by $3$. Therefore $n=15$ is divisible by $6$.''
- **Original Ans**: Affirming the consequent (or the converse error).

### A2
- **Original**: Name the fallacy: ``If $x>10$, then $x>0$. Suppose $x\leq10$. Therefore $x\leq0$.''
- **Original Ans**: Denying the antecedent (or the inverse error).

### A3
- **Original**: True or false: ``If a proof of $P\implies Q$ is shown to be flawed, then $P\implies Q$ must be false.''
- **Original Ans**: False.

### A4
- **Original**: A $6$-line ``proof'' is flawed only on line $4$. How many of the six lines can you actually trust?
- **Original Ans**: $3$ lines (Lines 1, 2, and 3).

### A6
- **Original**: A ``proof'' concludes $0=1$. What does this tell you about the argument?
- **Original Ans**: The argument must contain a flaw.

### A7
- **Original**: True or false: ``Verifying a universal claim $P(n)$ for $n=1,2,\dots,10$ constitutes a full proof...''
- **Original Ans**: False.

### A9
- **Original**: True or false: ``In a proof by contradiction, the final derived statement must be a strict logical contradiction...''
- **Original Ans**: False.

### B10
- **Original**: A student is told: ``It is not the case that every student in the class passed the exam.'' They conclude: ``Therefore, every student in the class failed the exam.'' What is wrong?
- **Original Ans**: Confused ``not all'' ($\exists x, \neg P(x)$) with ``none'' ($\forall x, \neg P(x)$).
